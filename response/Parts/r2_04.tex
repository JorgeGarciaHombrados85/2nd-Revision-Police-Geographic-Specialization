%Comment 4: Relatedly, criminals may easily cross municipal boundaries in Chile. Can you report the share of offenders apprehended in municipality X who committed crimes elsewhere? How should the reader think about this?




Thanks for the comment. If criminals cross municipal boundaries to commit crime, it is reasonable to expect that the implementation of \emph{Plan Cuadrante}, by increasing police effectiveness and deterrence, may have displaced some criminal activity from treated to untreated municipalities, biasing upward the estimated effects of the program.

Understanding the extent of that displacement is therefore important. We do not observe offenders' full travel patterns in Chile, so we cannot measure this directly. However, the institutional setting and the new spillover exercises both point to limited displacement. Chilean municipalities are large administrative units with an average area of about 2,000 square kilometers, making cross-boundary displacement costly. More importantly, in the revised manuscript we directly estimate spillover effects on crime, arrests, and police resources in neighboring untreated municipalities, and in the ENUSC subsample, and we find no evidence of meaningful spillovers in either direction. These analyses are described in detail in the response to the previous comment.
