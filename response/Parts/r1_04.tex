Thank you for the suggestion. We agree, and this point is closely related to our response to your first comment above: we should not frame our contribution as identifying the effect of local knowledge specifically, when the evidence instead supports the broader claim that the organization and territorial deployment of police resources matters for public safety.

We have edited two passages to reframe the contribution along the suggested sline: The abstract previously closed with: ``The beat-policing components of the reform---i.e., permanent officer assignment and greater local knowledge---appear to be key to reducing crime and improving security perceptions.'' We have edited it to show: ``The beat-policing components of the reform---the permanent, territorial reorganization of officer deployment---appear to be key to reducing crime and improving security perceptions.''

Similarly, the Conclusion opens with: ``The beat policing elements of the reform---permanent assignment to quadrants enabling officers to build local knowledge---seem to be key drivers of its effectiveness.'' We would revise this to: ``The beat policing elements of the reform---the permanent reorganization of officers into smaller territorial units---seem to be key drivers of its effectiveness, consistent with an organizational and deployment channel rather than local knowledge alone.''

We believe the Introduction and the Results section require no changes on this point, since neither frames local knowledge as the specific mechanism behind our estimates. Where the Introduction discusses our contribution, local knowledge appears only as one item in a list of design features---alongside police visibility and officers' adaptive capacity---while the language attributing the estimated effects is generic (``this model,'' ``these mechanisms,'' ``organizational improvements''), placing the focus on the territorial reorganization as a whole rather than on local knowledge specifically; the Results section does not attribute our estimates to any mechanism at all.
