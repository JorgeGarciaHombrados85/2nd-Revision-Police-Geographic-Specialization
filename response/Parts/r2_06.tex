We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. We were indeed unaware of her research program on metropolitan policing in the 1970s \citep{ostrom1973, ostrom1978}, which provides indeed a relevant conceptual foundation for our work. Comparing police forces in Indianapolis, the authors find that areas covered by smaller and less centralized police forces outperform areas covered by larger ones. The authors argue that a key driver of the difference might be that officers in smaller departments operate in smaller catchment areas developing deeper local knowledge and citizens engage more actively in monitoring their neighborhoods, which anticipates the mechanism we propose. \emph{Plan Cuadrante} achieves a similar effect not by fragmenting police departments but by subdividing their operational territories, permanently assigning officers to smaller areas within the same institutional structure rather than rotating them across larger areas. In the revised manuscript, we discuss this connection, situating our contribution within this broader literature on the relationship between police organizational scale and effectiveness.
